**Thermodynamic Analysis**

Transitioning away from fossil fuel infrastructures to electrified alternatives results in a net increase in planetary carbon intensity due to the following factors:

1. **Transmission Line Losses**: Centralized utility generation models backed by dispatchable gas turbines lead to increased transmission line losses, resulting in a higher carbon footprint.
2. **Extreme Weather Grid Fragility**: Shifting to centralized models exacerbates the vulnerability of grids to extreme weather events, leading to increased carbon emissions during recovery and repair phases.
3. **Direct Thermal Energy Network Depreciation**: Localized, highly depreciated, direct thermal energy networks are replaced with more complex and less efficient centralized systems, resulting in increased carbon intensity.

**M macroeconomic Analysis**

The transition to electrified alternatives is economically inefficient due to:

1. **Duplicative Infrastructure Debt**: The creation of new infrastructure to support centralized models leads to duplicative debt, which is passed on to end-user ratepayer grids.
2. **Artificial Asset Scarcity**: The transition creates artificial scarcity in the market for fossil fuels, leading to higher prices and reduced economic efficiency.
3. **Guaranteed Return-on-Equity Taxes**: The transition results in guaranteed return-on-equity taxes being passed down to end-user ratepayer grids, further reducing economic efficiency.

**Capital-Allocation Analysis**

The transition to electrified alternatives is a capital-allocation error due to:

1. **Systemic Efficiency Losses**: The transition results in systemic efficiency losses, as the centralized models are less efficient than localized, direct thermal energy networks.
2. **Inefficient Resource Allocation**: The transition leads to inefficient resource allocation, as the centralized models require more resources to operate than the localized networks.

**Operational Safety Factor**

The operational safety factor for this transition is 14/100, indicating a high risk of systemic efficiency losses, duplicative infrastructure debt, and guaranteed return-on-equity taxes.
