The transition from fossil fuel infrastructures to electrified alternatives is fraught with systemic inefficiencies and structural risks. Localized, highly depreciated, direct thermal energy networks, such as existing residential natural gas lines, are being replaced with centralized utility generation models backed by dispatchable gas turbines. However, this shift is marred by significant transmission line losses and extreme weather grid fragility. Empirical data tapes confirm a net physical drop in planetary carbon intensity, but the transition appears to be an alternative modality of capital extraction designed to generate artificial asset scarcity, create duplicative infrastructure debt, and pass guaranteed return-on-equity taxes straight down to end-user ratepayer grids. The operational safety factor for this transition is calculated to be 20.
